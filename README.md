## 📌 Introduction

This case study explores **Valve Corporation**—a company renowned for its flat organizational structure and strong culture of autonomy. Our objective was to design a cybersecurity strategy that strengthens Valve’s resilience without undermining its innovative work environment.

The result: a **Cybersecurity Center of Excellence (CoE)** tailored to Valve’s unique needs.

---

## 🧠 Project Overview

Valve is a leading force in gaming and digital distribution, yet its flat hierarchy presents challenges for implementing centralized cybersecurity protocols. Our approach involved:

1. **Organizational Analysis** – Understanding Valve’s decentralized, innovation-driven model.  
2. **SWOT Evaluation** – Identifying cybersecurity-related strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats.  
3. **Strategic Design** – Proposing a balanced solution: the **Cybersecurity Center of Excellence**.  
4. **Comparative Framework** – Drawing insights from **ENISA**, the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity.

---

## 🛡️ Proposed Solution: Cybersecurity Center of Excellence (CoE)

The CoE acts as a strategic, knowledge-sharing hub that:
- Provides **advisory services** without enforcing hierarchical control.
- Promotes **cybersecurity training** and company-wide awareness.
- **Collaborates externally** with expert bodies (e.g., ENISA).
- Facilitates innovation by securing Valve’s infrastructure transparently and respectfully.

---

## 🎯 CoE Key Objectives

- **Build Awareness** – Empower employees through continuous learning.
- **Enable Collaboration** – Coordinate cross-team security initiatives.
- **Drive Adaptation** – Evolve with emerging cybersecurity trends.

---

## 🧩 Why Valve?

Valve's flat culture demands thoughtful, non-intrusive solutions. Our CoE proposal preserves the company's core values—**freedom**, **creativity**, and **independence**—while elevating its cybersecurity maturity.

This strategy respects Valve’s structure and reinforces security through **trust**, not control.
